Track B Flagship Breakthrough Deep-Dive

GLM-5.1 exemplifies the next generation of scalable open‑weight language models through a meticulously designed Mixture‑of‑Experts (MoE) architecture. The model comprises 744B total parameters organized into 64 expert layers, each layer containing 128 experts; a learned router selects only two experts per token, yielding an active parameter count of roughly 40B. This sparse activation drastically reduces FLOPs per token compared to dense counterparts of similar size, enabling training on massive corpora with feasible compute budgets. To support the 200K‑token context window, GLM-5.1 employs rotary position embeddings (RoPE) with extended frequency bases and a hierarchical chunk‑wise attention mechanism that caches key/value states in GPU memory, minimizing recomputation during autoregressive generation. Latency‑compute tradeoffs are managed via expert‑level parallelism: experts are distributed across GPU nodes, and the router’s dispatch is overlapped with matrix multiplications, keeping per‑step latency comparable to 13B‑dense models while delivering far higher expressive power. The MIT‑licensed release of full weights democratizes access to this scale, allowing researchers to experiment with expert pruning, knowledge distillation, and specialized fine‑tuning without the prohibitive costs of training from scratch. Practically, GLM-5.1 pushes the frontier for long‑document understanding, few‑shot reasoning, and retrieval‑augmented workflows, setting a new standard for openness in frontier‑scale LLMs.
Track A: Top 5 Curated (Deterministic Heuristic Pipeline)
Procedural Graphs: Self-Evolving Execution Structures for LLM Agents
Research & Papers • Source: ArXiv • ✓ 200 OK
Introduces Procedural Graphs, a dynamic graph-based execution structure that allows LLM agents to self-modify their action plans over long horizons. By representing intermediate states and tool calls as evolving nodes and edges, the method enables adaptive replanning without unconstrained token generation. Experiments show improved success rates on multi-step tool-use benchmarks compared to baseline agents.
It's Not RoPE that Creates Sinks: The Role of Self-Concentration and Value-Non-Mixing in Attention
Research & Papers • Source: ArXiv • ✓ 200 OK
Challenges the common belief that Rotary Position Embeddings (RoPE) cause attention sinks and massive activations at the first token. Through ablation and theoretical analysis, the paper shows that self‑concentration of query/key vectors and lack of value mixing are the primary drivers. The findings suggest alternative architectural tweaks to mitigate sink effects and stabilize training of long‑context LLMs.
NOAH: Learning the Full Patient Journey. A Longitudinal Multimodal Time-Aware Model for Representation and Forecasting
Research & Papers • Source: ArXiv • ✓ 200 OK
Presents NOAH, a transformer‑based model that ingests irregularly sampled electronic health records, imaging, and lab results to learn a unified patient trajectory over a lifetime. The model incorporates time‑aware positional encodings and a hierarchical masking strategy to handle missing modalities. On benchmark forecasting tasks (e.g., disease onset, readmission), NOAH outperforms prior state‑of‑the‑art methods by significant margins.
DeCAL: Towards Physically-Grounded Dexterous Vision-Language-Action Models via Contact-Aware Latent Co-Imagination
Research & Papers • Source: ArXiv • ✓ 200 OK
Proposes DeCAL, a vision‑language‑action framework that learns to imagine contact‑rich interactions in a latent space before executing dexterous manipulations. By co‑imagining hand‑object contacts and visual features, the model reduces reliance on dense tactile sensing and improves generalization across novel objects and tasks. Real‑world robot experiments demonstrate higher success rates in fine‑grained assembly and tool use compared to prior VLA baselines.
MeClear: Cooperative Game-Theoretic Attribution and Risk-Aware Memory Clearance for Long-Horizon LLM Agents
Research & Papers • Source: ArXiv • ✓ 200 OK
Introduces MeClear, a principled method for deciding what to retain or discard in an LLM agent’s external memory over extended interactions. Using cooperative game theory, it assigns attribution scores to memory entries based on their contribution to future reward, while a risk‑aware component penalizes potentially harmful forgetting. Agents equipped with MeClear show better long‑term coherence and lower drift on multi‑session dialogue and task benchmarks.
